How it works

Strokes

  • DDing (Doum) — the deep bass note in the centre.
  • 1–9Tone fields — numbered from the lowest note upwards, zig-zagging left and right.
  • B1 B2Bottom notes (D Kurd 12) — on the underside of the shell.
  • TTak / Tek — a light fingertip tap on the shell next to the ding, usually right hand.
  • KKa — the same tap with the other hand, usually left.
  • SSlap — a sharp pop on the outer shoulder. There is one on each side: SL left hand, SR right hand.

Blue notes = left hand Amber notes = right hand

Modes

Listen plays the song so you can sit back and enjoy it. Practice scores every stroke from the first note (Perfect, Good, Miss). Wait for note lets the notes fly in and then stops on each one until you play it — play in time and it never stops. Tuner listens to your handpan and shows how many cents each note is from A4 = 440 Hz.

Speed goes down to 0.1×. Use [ and ] to change it while you play.

Song tape

Under the handpan the strokes scroll past as numbers, a little of what you just played and more of what comes next. Chords are stacked. In Practice and Wait for note each stroke turns teal for Perfect, violet for Good and red for Miss. Press Tour to watch the demo tour again.

Keyboard

Space play / pause · D ding · 1–9 tone fields · 0 / - bottoms · T K S strokes (Shift+S right slap) · [ ] speed

Microphone (beta)

Turn the microphone on and play your real handpan: tone fields are recognised by pitch, and any clear percussive hit counts for T, K or S. Turn the song sound (speaker button) off or use headphones so the app does not hear itself.

Writing songs

4L 8R - | 4L+2R 8R - — space separates strokes, + plays together, L/R picks the hand, :0.5 makes a stroke half a beat, - is a one-beat rest, | is a bar line.
